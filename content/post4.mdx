## Project Overview

This project explores how sentiment analysis of social media chatter can sharpen ad targeting. To do this, I took on the role of a data scientist at Valve, the company behind CS:GO, and approached the problem as if I were helping the company improve its ad targeting strategy.

The goal: help a gaming company (like Valve) target ads more effectively based on player sentiment.

## Executive Summary for Stakeholders

Social media sentiment reflects how users feel about a product or brand. I built a model that can detect this sentiment from tweets and used it to inform a marketing strategy for CS:GO. This includes:

- Understanding common complaints about competitors
- Segmenting audiences by their sentiment toward these games
- Building a custom classifier to identify potential converts in real time

With a test accuracy of **~90%**, the sentiment model can be deployed to monitor live Twitter data and inform marketing decisions day-to-day.

## Key Findings & Recommendations

### 1. CS:GO has a strong fanbase but cheating is a major complaint

The most common negative keywords in CS:GO tweets were about _cheaters_ and _hackers_. This aligns with known community complaints. Valorant, for example, gained significant traction by prioritizing anti-cheat technology, and its player base grew from **1.7M to over 3M daily active users within a year** of its release.

**Recommendation:**

- Promote anti-cheat improvements in marketing
- Share data on bans and upgrades publicly to rebuild trust

### 2. Twitch and skins are central to CS:GO's positive sentiment

Users love giveaways and Twitch content. Live events and skins are part of the culture.

**Recommendation:**

- Partner with streamers for exclusive giveaways
- Promote Twitch drops during major tournaments

Twitch drops have been shown to **double or triple concurrent viewership** and player engagement for games like Apex Legends and Valorant.

### 3. Discontent is high among competitor audiences

Many users expressed frustration with:

- **Call of Duty (51.8% negative)** - Update fatigue and claims the game is "dead"
- **PUBG (50.7% negative)** - Banned in India and restricted in China
- **Fortnite / Rainbow Six (48-49% negative)** - Bugs and lack of fixes

**Recommendation:**

- Highlight CS:GO’s smaller updates, stable performance, and strong concurrency numbers
- Emphasize CS:GO’s availability in India and China, where PUBG’s absence left a large void

PUBG had **33+ million monthly active users in India alone** before the ban.

## Projected Business Impact

Let’s estimate the value this system could bring:

- CS:GO has approximately **32 million monthly active users (MAU)** as of 2024
- If the model identifies **5%** of these users as expressing negative sentiments toward competitor games, that's **1.6 million potential ad targets per month**
- If targeted ads convert just **2%** of them, that's **32,000 new players per month**

Steam's estimated average revenue per user (ARPU) is **~\$19** (via [Statista](https://www.statista.com/topics/4282/steam/#topicOverview)). Since CS:GO is a significant part of Steam's revenue, this provides a general estimate for CS:GO's ARPU as well which can be used to estimate:

- Estimated monthly revenue: 32,000 new players \* \$19 ARPU = **\$608,000 additional revenue per month**
- Estimated annual revenue: \$608,000 \* 12 months = **\$7.3 million additional revenue per year**

These estimates are based on publicly available data and conservative assumptions to provide a realistic projection of potential business impact.
