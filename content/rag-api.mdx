Sprout powers the AI chat in the [RENEW scleroderma app](/blog/renew-app). The codebase is private (Michigan Medicine). The approach is documented in a peer-reviewed paper: [JMIR mHealth and uHealth, 2026;14:e79302](https://doi.org/10.2196/79302).

## Purpose

RENEW's AI health coach answers questions from people managing a chronic illness. Answers have to be grounded in vetted clinical and patient-education content, written in plain language, and stay inside coaching boundaries — no diagnoses, no medical advice beyond the source material. I was hired in June 2025 to design and build the retrieval system that makes that possible.

## Outcomes

- A LangGraph pipeline in production behind the app's chat: classify the message, retrieve if needed, generate with citations, or respond directly for non-clinical turns.
- Hybrid retrieval over a corpus of ~112 clinical and patient-education documents: dense vectors (pgvector) and sparse keyword search fused with Reciprocal Rank Fusion, then listwise LLM reranking with a relevance threshold.
- A personalization layer beyond plain RAG: per-user clinical, conversation, and check-in summaries, plus a coaching-hypothesis engine that forms and corrects hypotheses about each user across sessions.
- Five evaluation systems (below) — the part of this work I now spend most of my time on.

## Features

- **Retrieval** — markdown-aware chunking that keeps section headers with each chunk; query rewriting at the classification stage; checksum-deduped ingestion tagged into six clinical domains.
- **Safety** — role-locked system prompt, instruction-injection resistance for retrieved documents, behavioral-only coaching boundaries, and reading-level enforcement targeting an 8th-grade score.
- **Memory** — conversation history via a Postgres checkpointer, with per-user summaries injected by intent.

## Evaluation

How do you know a medical chatbot is actually good? One metric can't answer that, so Sprout has five evaluation systems:

1. **Retrieval metrics** — human-verified gold labels; Recall@k, Precision@k, MRR, nDCG, and AP with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals, plus threshold sweeps to tune retrieval parameters.
2. **Blinded pairwise judging** — an LLM judge compares responses with and without personalization context, blinded to which is which, scoring naturalness and persona fit.
3. **Multi-turn scenario evals** — scripted multi-session conversations run through the real session-end summarization and hypothesis passes, scoring whether the system's picture of the user converges on the truth without contamination.
4. **Memory bake-offs** — the custom hypothesis system benchmarked against LangMem and mem0.
5. **Clinical-accuracy evals** — a DeepEval suite (RAG triad, safety-gated rubrics, a custom reading-level metric) plus a promptfoo suite judged by a different model family than the one generating, to avoid self-evaluation bias.

## Tooling

Python 3.13, FastAPI, LangGraph and LangChain, PostgreSQL with pgvector, Docker Compose, systemd on a university Linux server, DeepEval, promptfoo. Models are served through UM-GPT, the university's OpenAI-compatible gateway.

## Behind the Scenes

The hybrid search shipped with a fix I had to write myself: the library's Reciprocal Rank Fusion ranked the dense leg incorrectly, so Sprout carries a corrected implementation. And the judge-model choice matters more than it looks — early evals judged by the same model family that generated the answers scored consistently higher than evals judged by a different family, which is exactly the bias the literature warns about.
