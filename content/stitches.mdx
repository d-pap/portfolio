## Purpose

The STITCHES Doll Project, founded in 1999, preserves the stories of women affected by HIV/AIDS, domestic violence, bleeding disorders, and other serious issues. Each woman makes a doll that answers one question: *"If your doll could talk, what would she say?"* The dolls travel as a national exhibit.

The website was built in 2016 with a desktop website builder: 105 hand-made pages, 1,261 images, image-based navigation buttons, no mobile layout, and no HTTPS. I'm rebuilding it from the ground up as a freelance project — design and build.

## Where it stands

The content migration is done and the project is in the design phase. So far:

- ~550 doll records extracted from the old static HTML with a custom Python pipeline, now living as structured, schema-validated content.
- A working Astro build with a page for every doll, gallery and program pages, and an interactive map of where dolls come from.
- 87 redirects mapped from old URLs so nothing breaks at cutover.
- A git-based CMS configured so the founder can edit content through simple forms — no code, no hosting bill.

## Design direction

Two candidate directions, to be presented to the client as live previews rather than static mockups: a "handstitched" theme built around red-thread accents drawn from the dolls themselves, and a minimal gallery treatment that puts the photography first. Both are storytelling-first and accessibility-minded.

## Tooling

Astro, TypeScript, hand-written CSS with design tokens, content collections with Zod validation, Pages CMS, Leaflet with OpenStreetMap, Cloudflare Pages. Hosting cost: about $0/month.

## Behind the Scenes

The old site had no export, no database, and no source files — just rendered HTML. The migration pipeline parses every page, extracts each doll's photo, story, and origin, and writes them into structured JSON. Getting 550 records out of 105 hand-built pages was the least glamorous and most valuable part of the project.
