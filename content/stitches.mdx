## overview

The Stitches Doll Project began in 1999 as a way for women affected by HIV/AIDS to tell their stories through handmade dolls. The collection grew to include experiences of violence, bleeding disorders, and other challenges.

I'm rebuilding the website for the project's founder, Kathy. I handle the design, development, and migration from the old site.

## rebuilding the archive

The original site was a collection of static HTML pages. I wrote a Python pipeline to extract the doll records and turn them into structured content with schema validation.

Then came the work that a parser can't settle: matching photographs to the right dolls, separating shared entries, and reviewing which images need retaking. I built local review tools to make those decisions easier.

## designing for reading

The stories are the reason to visit. The gallery lets people filter a collection and read it in rows, with longer stories expandable in place. Each doll also has a detail page.

The selected filters stay with you as you move between dolls. Returning to the gallery restores your place, so reading a collection doesn't mean repeatedly starting over.

The visual direction is warm and handmade, with the dolls' own photographs doing most of the work.

## implementation

Astro generates the site from typed content collections. Pages CMS gives Kathy an editing interface, and Cloudflare Pages hosts the build.

I've also mapped redirects from the old pages for the eventual move. The rebuild is still in development; the existing public site hasn't been replaced yet.
