## Summary

Social media platforms like Twitter provide real-time customer feedback. In this project, I analyzed over **61,000 tweets** about CS:GO and its competitors, built a sentiment analysis model with **94.7% accuracy**, and identified key insights into what drives player dissatisfaction or loyalty. These insights can significantly enhance how gaming companies target their ads, attract new players, and retain existing ones.

## Key Insights & Recommendations

I approached this analysis as if I were a data scientist at Valve, presenting to their marketing team. Here are the main insights and actionable recommendations based on the analysis:

### 1. CS:GO has a strong fanbase but cheating is a major complaint

The most common negative keywords in CS:GO tweets were about _cheaters_ and _hackers_. This aligns with known community complaints. Valorant, for example, gained significant traction by prioritizing anti-cheat technology, and its player base grew from **1.7M to over 3M daily active users** within a year of its release.

**Recommendation:**

- Publicly highlight Valve's anti-cheat improvements
- Regularly share data on bans and upgrades to build transparency and trust

### 2. Twitch and cosmetic items drive CS:GO's positive sentiment

Users love giveaways and Twitch content. Live events and cosmetic items ("skins") are part of the culture. Games like Apex Legends and Valorant have seen their active player counts **double or even triple** due to Twitch drops and streamer partnerships.

**Recommendation:**

- Invest in partnerships with streamers
- Promote Twitch drops during major tournaments

### 3. High frustration among competitor audiences

Many users expressed frustration with:

- **Call of Duty (51.8% negative)** - Update fatigue and claims the game is "dead"
- **PUBG (50.7% negative)** - Banned in India and restricted in China
- **Fortnite / Rainbow Six (48-49% negative)** - Bugs and lack of fixes

PUBG had **33+ million monthly active users in India alone** before the ban.

**Recommendation:**

- Highlight CS:GO’s smaller updates, stable performance, and strong concurrency numbers
- Emphasize CS:GO’s availability in India and China, where PUBG’s absence left a large void

## Potential Business Impact

To clearly demonstrate the financial benefit of implementing targeted sentiment-driven advertising, here’s a simplified, realistic scenario:

Step 1: Audience size:

- Suppose Valve targets social media users expressing frustration with competitors.
- From roughly **32 million monthly active CS:GO users** (ActivePlayer.io, 2024), a conservative assumption is that Valve’s marketing efforts could reach **5% (1.6 million)** of users who are dissatisfied with competitor games.

Step 2: Conversion assumption:

- With targeted and relevant ads, it's reasonable to assume **2%** of these users could be converted into new or returning active CS:GO players each month. This is based on typical digital marketing benchmarks for personalized and relevant campaigns (HubSpot Marketing Benchmarks, 2023).
- Thus, monthly new players gained: **1.6 million users x 2% conversion = 32,000 new players/month**

Step 3: Revenue impact (reasonable assumption):

- CS:GO primarily generates revenue through cosmetic items and keys, with estimated average revenue per paying user (ARPPU) around **\$5 per month** (Newzoo Cosmetics Report, 2023).
- Monthly revenue gain: **32,000 players × \$5 ARPPU = \$160,000 per month**
- Annual revenue gain: **\$160,000 × 12 months = \$1.92 million annually**

Even with conservative assumptions, leveraging sentiment analysis to better target frustrated competitor audiences can yield a significant revenue uplift.
